v1.12.2 (expected around 2027-03-01)
====================

- ``RotateAndTiltWrapperPotential`` can move and turn in time: ``center=``
  places the wrapped potential's origin at a constant position, along a
  tabulated path ``(t, xyz)``, or on an integrated ``Orbit``, and
  ``orientation=`` gives its rotation as a function of time, tabulated as
  unit quaternions or rotation matrices (interpolated as quaternions with
  natural cubic splines and normalized after interpolation;
  ``RotateAndTiltWrapperPotential.orientation_from_angles`` tabulates it from
  angles), so that the potential is ``pot(R(t) (x - center(t)) + offset, t)``.
  Any wrapped potential, including non-axisymmetric and time-dependent ones,
  in Python and in C (including the variational equations). An integer
  ``zvec`` now works, and ``zvec`` is no longer normalized in place (also
  for the triaxial potentials).

- ``MovingObjectPotential`` supports non-axisymmetric potentials, which move
  with the object as ``RotateAndTiltWrapperPotential(pot=pot, center=orbit)``
  (axisymmetric potentials keep their existing implementation).

- ``actionAngleVerticalInverse`` can be set up from the maximum height of its
  largest torus: ``zmax=`` (with ``nE=`` tori) builds the tori between the
  orbit at rest and the one that reaches ``zmax``, spaced uniformly in the
  speed with which they cross the midplane, as an alternative to the grid of
  energies ``Es=``; with ``setup_interp=True`` every torus that stays below
  ``zmax`` is then available. In a disk's vertical potential this spacing
  is several to a few tens of times more accurate between its tori than energies spaced
  uniformly, with the same number of tori.

- ``actionAngleStaeckelInverse`` evaluates arrays of actions, broadcast
  against the angles by numpy's rules like the vertical and spherical
  inverses (scalar actions with arrays of angles: one torus at those angles;
  arrays of actions with scalar angles: that angle on every torus; equal
  shapes: each point on its own torus at its own angle; actions of shape
  (n,1) with angles of shape (m,): every angle on every torus), in ``__call__``,
  ``xvFreqs`` and ``Freqs``, with the tables read once per distinct torus;
  a single torus takes angles of any shape and returns its frequencies as
  floats. The distinct tori of an interpolated family are evaluated in one
  vectorized pass (batched interpolation of the tables, label inversion and
  angle solve), about eighty times faster than one torus at a time.

- ``actionAngleSphericalInverse`` evaluates arrays two to four times faster
  and ``actionAngleVerticalInverse`` somewhat faster: the harmonics of the
  maps come from one sine and cosine per point by angle addition, the
  spherical inverse's angle solve converges superlinearly, and its tables
  are read for all tori at once.

- ``actionAngleIsochroneInverse`` broadcasts arrays of actions against the
  angles by numpy's rules, like the other inverses: before, arrays of actions
  had to have the shape of the angles, and an array of actions at a scalar
  angle, or actions of shape (n,1) with angles of shape (m,), raised.

- ``actionAngleSphericalInverse`` and ``actionAngleVerticalInverse`` have a
  public ``E`` method, the inverse of their ``Jr(E, L)`` and ``J(E)``: the
  energy of the torus with the given actions, for arrays of actions, from the
  family's own action table (so that ``E(Jr(E, L), L)`` is ``E`` to round-off)
  or, for explicit tori, from the set-up tori; it returns a Quantity when the
  instance has physical units.
- ``actionAngleSphericalInverse`` and ``actionAngleStaeckelInverse`` take the
  spacing of their angular-momentum grid as an option (``Lspacing`` and
  ``Lzspacing``: 'linear', the default, 'sqrt' or 'log'). A linear grid
  that reaches a small ``Rmin`` has an innermost cell spanning a large
  factor in angular momentum, which holds the family's most eccentric tori
  and most of its interpolation error; spaced in the logarithm, that cell's
  action error drops by three orders of magnitude on an eight-node grid, at
  the price of a coarser outer grid.

- ``actionAngleVerticalInverse`` samples the momentum of each torus through
  ``actionAngleVertical``'s radicand, so that the smallest tori of a vertical
  potential measured from the midplane (below about 70 pc in height for
  ``MWPotential2014``'s) are sampled to full precision, their anomaly maps
  converge without the warning they used to raise, and their round trip
  through the forward transformation holds (the anomaly-map fit is also
  made dimensionless, since its absolute tolerances used to declare a tiny
  torus converged before fitting anything); the actions, frequencies and
  turning points of a family's tori are computed in one vectorized call, and
  the torus at the potential's minimum takes the harmonic frequency.

- ``actionAngleVertical`` evaluates arrays of points at once: the turning
  points by a vectorized bisection and the action, frequency and angle by
  fixed-order Gauss-Legendre quadrature in the variable sqrt(xmax - x), in
  which the integrands are regular at the turning point, instead of an
  adaptive quadrature per point (a hundred times faster for a population,
  agreeing with the previous results to 1e-12). Near the turning point, and
  throughout a torus too small for the potential's own round-off, E - Phi is
  computed from the integral of the force instead of a difference of
  potentials, so that the smallest tori of a vertical potential measured
  from the midplane keep their digits.

- The cylindrical-to-confocal transformation (``coords.Rz_to_uv``,
  ``Rz_to_coshucosv``, and its C counterparts in the Staeckel action-angle
  code and the Staeckel wrapper) computes sinh u and sin v (prolate) or
  cos v (oblate) in forms without cancellation. The former cosh u = (d_1 +
  d_2)/(2 delta) and cos v = (d_1 - d_2)/(2 delta) lost the small coordinate
  near the axis and the plane: u was off by a part in 1e9 at R = 1e-4 delta
  and zero at R = 1e-12 delta, and on the axis beyond a focus v was NaN. All
  points, including the foci, the focal segment, the axis, and the plane, now
  transform to round-off, and the Staeckel wrapper's in-plane force equals the
  wrapped potential's to round-off at every radius.

- The isochrone auxiliary of ``actionAngleSphericalInverse`` and
  ``actionAngleStaeckelInverse`` is now fitted to the potential's rotation
  curve by a converged least squares (the amplitude eliminated in closed
  form, Newton's method in the logarithm of the scale radius, from the
  geometric mean of the sampled radii) instead of a Nelder-Mead simplex: the
  fit is exact for an isochrone target, takes a millisecond, and no longer
  depends on a starting point; families built with the fitted auxiliary move
  at the level of the old simplex's tolerance (a few 1e-5 relative in the
  auxiliary's parameters, 1e-12 in the returned phase-space points), far
  below their interpolation accuracy.

- ``actionAngleVerticalInverse`` (in its default, momentum-matched mode) and
  ``actionAngleSphericalInverse`` evaluate arrays of actions: the actions and
  the angles broadcast against each other, so that a population of stars,
  each on its own torus, is mapped to phase space (``__call__``,
  ``xvFreqs``) or to its frequencies (``Freqs``) in one call, with the
  tables of each distinct torus read once and every solve (the family's
  label inversion, the anomaly of each angle) run over all points at once;
  a single torus keeps its scalar frequencies.

- ``actionAngleIsochroneInverse`` carries the closed forms of the isochrone's
  tori (energy, radial action, the orbital parameters (a, e) with their
  first and second partials along the actions, and the radius, momentum,
  flux and cumulative radial action in the eccentric anomaly with their
  partials) as private methods, which the spherical and Staeckel inverses
  use for the auxiliary tori they lift onto; its own evaluation now takes
  (a, e) and the radial profile from these, with the eccentricity in a
  product form that is exact down to the circular orbit (a round-off-level
  change), and is split at Kepler's equation so that a caller that knows the
  eccentric anomaly already, as the spherical inverse does from its anomaly
  map, does not solve it again.

- The cylindrical-to-confocal transformation (``coords.Rz_to_uv``,
  ``Rz_to_coshucosv``, and its C counterparts in the Staeckel action-angle
  code and the Staeckel wrapper) computes sinh u and sin v (prolate) or
  cos v (oblate) in forms without cancellation. The former cosh u = (d_1 +
  d_2)/(2 delta) and cos v = (d_1 - d_2)/(2 delta) lost the small coordinate
  near the axis and the plane: u was off by a part in 1e9 at R = 1e-4 delta
  and zero at R = 1e-12 delta, and on the axis beyond a focus v was NaN. All
  points, including the foci, the focal segment, the axis, and the plane, now
  transform to round-off, and the Staeckel wrapper's in-plane force equals the
  wrapped potential's to round-off at every radius.

- OblateStaeckelWrapperPotential's derivatives along its U reference curve
  (dU/du and d2U/du2, Python and C) now use the exact dR/du = delta cosh u
  sin v0 and dz/du = delta sinh u cos v0 in place of a guarded R/tanh(u),
  whose guard biased the wrapper's force by a part in 1e12 / tanh(u): a part
  in 1e12 at R ~ delta, a part in 1e9 at R = 1e-3 delta. In the plane the
  wrapper's force now equals the wrapped potential's to round-off. The axis
  nodes of the tabulated (``ntab=``) dU/du and d2U/du2 are now extrapolated
  from the next three knots rather than evaluated at the axis, where the
  exact values (a vanishing first derivative, the finite part of a second
  derivative that diverges for a cusped potential) left the tables wrong
  over their first intervals: at ``ntab=100`` the tabulated Hessian of a
  smooth disc was off by a third at R = 0.05 delta and the tabulated force
  of a cusped bulge by a quarter at R = 0.02; both are now at spline
  accuracy for a smooth potential and within a few percent for a cusp.
  Where the force vanishes at the centre, as a smooth potential's does, the
  dU/du node keeps its exact value, zero (the extrapolation misses it by its
  cubic term), so the tabulated force of a smooth potential is right down to
  the axis: a part in 1e6 at R = 1e-5 at ``ntab=100``.

- Ellipsoidal potentials (TriaxialNFWPotential, PerfectEllipsoidPotential,
  ...) failed to evaluate their forces and second derivatives, and with them
  epifreq, at an array of R with a scalar z.

- Fixed the check for a new galpy version on PyPI, which silently stopped
  working with recent pip versions; it now uses PyPI's JSON API.

- Orbit.integrate and Orbit.integrate_dxdv now raise a clear TypeError when
  the times are given as a list (use an array); lists of times were documented
  as supported, but had stopped working.

- Fixed Orbit.animate, which raised ``ValueError: unexpected '{' in field
  name`` in v1.12.1.

- New ``actionAngleSphericalInverse``: the inverse action-angle transformation
  for spherical potentials, through the same momentum-matched canonical map
  as the one-dimensional case. Each torus is sampled exactly in its radial
  anomaly and mapped onto the torus of equal (J_r, L) of a frozen isochrone
  auxiliary by the radial point transformation that matches cumulative
  radial actions (a sine series in the anomaly, ``mm_npt`` harmonics fitted
  to ``mm_nta`` samples), lifted to the momenta cotangent-consistently; the
  polar libration needs no map because it does not depend on the potential,
  and the radial and polar angles carry the closed-form compensation for
  the moving turning points. Evaluation goes through the analytic isochrone
  inverse and undoes the lift; the map is exactly symplectic for any stored
  tables because every derivative it needs is that of the stored
  interpolant itself. Tori are given as explicit (E, L) pairs or as an
  (E, L) grid with ``setup_interp=True`` (angular momenta of the circular
  orbits between ``Rmin`` and ``Rmax``, energies up to the potential at
  ``Rinf``, uniform in the logarithm of the radial orbit's apocentre --
  which is the energy itself for a logarithmic halo, and in a potential
  with an escape energy, where every apocentre diverges, compresses the
  spacing toward the top so that the tables stay smooth up to it -- and
  quadratically spaced at the circular edge), with ``Jr(E, L)``
  giving a torus's radial action; ``Freqs`` returns the frequencies of the
  returned orbits; the isochrone auxiliary is fitted to the potential's
  rotation curve unless given (``auxiliary=``), and is the ``auxiliary``
  attribute either way. The family's tables are Hermite interpolants (quintic
  along the energy variable, cubic along L) whose first partials are exact
  at every node: the energy is analytic in the grid's variables, the
  radial action's partials follow from the node's own frequencies, the
  turning points' from the level-set rule, and the anomaly map's from the
  variation of the matching condition on each torus alone, so a grid
  torus's angles and frequencies are exact and the family between its
  nodes is accurate to ~1e-7 in angle on a 16 x 16 grid. The evaluation is
  a single vectorized pass in the anomaly: the auxiliary anomaly and radial
  angle, the target's radius and radial momentum, and the compensations
  come from the tables in closed form, Newton runs on the anomaly, and the
  three-dimensional reconstruction is one call of the analytic isochrone
  inverse for all points (0.02 ms per point). The auxiliary's cumulative
  action, which the matching inverts, is closed form (the two arctangents
  of the isochrone's angle relation, with their linear parts gathered so
  that the round-off is the action's own), as are its parameter partials
  that the map's slopes need; the map's projection and evaluation on the
  anomaly grid are FFTs, and the slopes are solved by a Galerkin projection
  onto the sine basis formed by FFT, so a family's construction costs
  nothing beyond its samples: 2 s for a 16 x 16 grid, and a minute at a
  thousand harmonics; ``progressbar=`` (default True) shows tqdm bars over
  the tori. The circular orbits are the
  bottom row of the grid, with the epicycle limit's exact partials, so the
  family reaches J_r -> 0 continuously, and the circular orbit itself
  (J_r = 0) evaluates in closed form with the epicycle and circular
  frequencies, in a grid or as an explicit torus. A warning is raised when the anomaly map does not
  resolve a torus, and a torus that reaches beyond the depth of the fitted
  auxiliary raises. An explicit
  torus carries the second-order tables of its one-node family (the
  turning points' and the anomaly map's second derivatives from the
  level-set rule and the second variation of the matching condition, and
  the frequencies' derivatives from the period quadratures, all from the
  torus alone), so that the map's derivatives in the actions at the torus
  are the true family's.

- New ``actionAngleStaeckelInverse``: the inverse action-angle transformation
  for axisymmetric Staeckel potentials, through the same momentum-matched
  canonical map as the spherical case, applied to each of a torus's two
  librations: the u and v librations in the prolate spheroidal coordinates
  of the potential's focal length are sampled exactly in their anomalies
  (turning points and momenta from the separated Hamilton-Jacobi equation)
  and mapped onto the torus of equal (J_R, L = J_z + |L_z|, L_z) of a frozen
  isochrone auxiliary, u onto the auxiliary's radial libration and v onto
  its polar one, each by the point transformation that matches cumulative
  actions (a sine series in the anomaly of ``mm_npt`` harmonics fitted to
  ``mm_nta`` samples; the v map's odd harmonics vanish by the symmetry
  about the midplane), lifted to the momenta cotangent-consistently. The
  three angles carry the closed-form compensation for the moving turning
  points of both librations; the map is exactly symplectic for any stored
  tables because every derivative it needs is that of the stored
  interpolant itself. The potential must supply its focal length (an
  ``OblateStaeckelWrapperPotential`` or an exact Staeckel potential such as
  ``KuzminKutuzovStaeckelPotential``), or ``delta=`` wraps a general
  axisymmetric potential in the Staeckel approximation. Tori are given as
  explicit (E, L_z, I_3) triples (the third integral in the convention of
  the separated momenta with the gauge V(pi/2) = 0) or as a grid with
  ``setup_interp=True`` (angular momenta of the circular orbits between
  ``Rmin`` and ``Rmax``, energies from the circular orbit's to that of the
  planar orbit with apocentre ``Rinf``, uniform in the logarithm of the
  apocentre of the planar orbit without angular momentum -- the energy
  itself for a logarithmic potential, and a spacing compressed toward the
  top where an escape energy makes every apocentre diverge -- third
  integrals from the planar orbit's to the shell orbit's, quadratically
  spaced at every degenerate edge), with ``JR(E, Lz, I3)`` and ``Jz(E,
  Lz, I3)`` giving a torus's
  actions; ``Freqs`` returns the frequencies of the returned orbits; the
  isochrone auxiliary is fitted to the potential's rotation curve unless
  given (``auxiliary=``), and is the ``auxiliary`` attribute either way.
  The family's tables are Hermite interpolants in three variables (quintic
  along the energy variable, cubic along the other two) whose first
  partials are exact at every node: the actions' from the node's period
  matrix, the turning points' from the level-set rule, and the anomaly
  maps' from the variation of the matching condition on each torus alone,
  chained through the grid's analytic (E, I_3)(w_E, w_I, L_z) relation;
  the degenerate faces (circular, shell, and planar orbits) carry their
  harmonic limits, so the family reaches vanishing actions continuously,
  and the circular orbit itself evaluates in closed form with the epicycle,
  circular, and vertical frequencies. Evaluation solves the two angle
  relations for the anomaly pair by a triangular Newton iteration on the
  auxiliary's angles, reconstructs the point from the two librations, and
  takes the azimuth from the auxiliary's angle relations. A warning is
  raised when a map does not resolve a torus (the u map needs harmonics of
  the order of a torus's ratio of outer to inner radius in the plane, the
  v map of the order of L / |L_z| as the torus approaches a polar orbit),
  and a torus that reaches beyond the depth of the fitted auxiliary raises
  with the same advice. As for the spherical inverse, the auxiliary's
  cumulative actions are closed forms with their linear parts gathered, the
  maps' projections and evaluations on the anomaly grid are FFTs, and the
  slopes are solved by a Galerkin projection; ``progressbar=`` (default
  True) shows tqdm bars over the tori. ``u0=`` accepts a callable
  ``u0(E, Lz)`` or ``'fit'`` to give every (E, L_z) of a family its own
  reference curve in the Staeckel approximation of a general potential
  (``'fit'`` places it at the harmonic mean of the pericentre and apocentre
  of the planar orbits at that (E, L_z)),
  so that each torus is a torus of its own best-placed Staeckel model,
  which matters for families spanning a wide radial range; the reference
  curve is construction-only gauge (the map's (R, z) <-> (u, v) uses the
  focal length alone), so this costs nothing at evaluation. ``target=``
  localizes a family's grid on the tori of a set of phase-space points (an
  Orbit or rows (R, vR, vT, z, vz)): the grid's box in (L_z, w_E, w_I) is
  then the points' own range, padded by ``target_pad`` times its spread
  and at least ``target_minwidth`` of the default span wide, so that the
  same number of nodes resolves a target orbit or stream far better than
  the default box does. An explicit torus carries the second-order tables
  of its one-node family (the second derivatives of its turning points and
  of both anomaly maps' coefficients and the period matrix's derivatives
  in (E, I_3, L_z), all from the torus alone), so that the map's
  derivatives in the actions at the torus are the true family's.

v1.12.1 (2026-11-02)
====================

- Added ``TruncatedNFWPotential`` and ``TruncatedHernquistPotential``: the NFW
  and Hernquist profiles times the truncation of Baltz, Marshall & Oguri
  (2009), ``(rc^2 / (rc^2 + r^2))^n`` for any positive integer ``n``, with
  closed-form mass, potential, forces, and second derivatives accurate to
  ~1e-14. The truncation radius can depend on time (``tgrid=``), e.g., for a
  satellite that is stripped from the outside in, used as the ``pot`` of a
  ``MovingObjectPotential``. Both have C implementations (including
  ``integrate_dxdv``) and can be used with the spherical distribution
  functions.

- Added time-dependent support to ``SCFPotential``: the expansion coefficients
  ``Acos`` and ``Asin`` can be functions of time, given as callables or as
  arrays sampled on a ``tgrid``, interpolated in time with a cubic spline, with
  fast evaluation in Python and C. ``SCFPotential.from_density`` likewise
  accepts a ``tgrid`` to build a potential from a time-dependent density
  (analogous to the time-dependent ``MultipoleExpansionPotential``).

- Added ``SCFPotential.from_nbody`` to build an ``SCFPotential`` directly from
  particle positions and masses. Multiple snapshots with a ``tgrid`` build a
  time-dependent potential, and the particle sum is accumulated in batches, so
  memory use stays bounded for any number of particles.

- Added ``SCFPotential.from_multipole`` and
  ``MultipoleExpansionPotential.from_scf`` to translate between the two
  expansions. Because both expand in the same real spherical harmonics, the
  translation is purely radial; it supports time-dependent potentials.

- ``Orbit.integrate_dxdv`` now works with the symplectic C integrators
  (``leapfrog_c``, ``symplec4_c``, ``symplec6_c``), which propagate the
  deviations with the exact tangent map of each step, so the state-transition
  matrix is symplectic to machine precision. ``integrate_dxdv`` now also
  supports 1D orbits, with both the Runge-Kutta and the symplectic integrators.

- Added an ``isConservative`` attribute to all potentials and forces, True when
  the force derives from a (possibly time-dependent) potential. The symplectic
  integrators now fall back to a non-symplectic integrator only for forces that
  are not conservative, so they can, e.g., integrate orbits in a
  ``NonInertialFrameForce`` frame that accelerates without rotating.

- ``streamspraydf.streamTrack`` and ``StreamTrack.from_particles`` have a new
  default reconstruction, ``method='pspline'``: the particles' offsets from the
  progenitor's orbit are fit with a penalized cubic spline, with each
  coordinate's penalty chosen by restricted maximum likelihood. The track is
  now a smooth function of the particles and the progenitor, and for large
  samples the fit takes about half the time and a quarter of the memory.
  ``smoothing=`` and ``smoothing_s`` are now the 6 per-coordinate penalty
  weights; the previous reconstruction remains available as
  ``method='binned'``.

- ``actionAngleVerticalInverse`` now evaluates through a momentum-matched
  canonical map by default, whose auxiliary torus is the harmonic oscillator of
  the same action. The map, its momentum, and the angle are closed form, so the
  map is exactly symplectic, preserves the requested action to ~1e-13, and
  ``Freqs`` returns exactly the frequency of the returned orbits; the family of
  tori can be as small as a single torus. The previous evaluation remains
  available with ``momentum_matched=False``. Also fixes the frequency of the
  zero-energy grid torus.

- Added an exact point transformation to ``actionAngleVerticalInverse``
  (``use_pointtransform="exact"``), which maps each torus exactly onto its
  auxiliary harmonic-oscillator torus by quadrature along the orbit, so the
  point transformation alone is the complete torus mapping. With
  ``pt_only=True``, the inverse transformation is evaluated with the point
  transformation alone (much faster).

- ``actionAngleVerticalInverse`` now supports physical units (``ro=``,
  ``vo=``, and Quantity inputs and outputs).

- ``OblateStaeckelWrapperPotential`` is ~3x faster in C, and the new ``ntab=``
  option tabulates its functions along the reference curves as splines for
  ~15x faster C orbit integration (agreeing with the exact evaluation to
  ~2e-8 at ``ntab=3000``).

- ``actionAngleStaeckel`` no longer needs ``delta=`` for a potential that
  supplies its own focal length: the exact Staeckel potentials
  ``KuzminKutuzovStaeckelPotential`` and the axisymmetric oblate (b = 1,
  c < 1) ``PerfectEllipsoidPotential``, and
  ``OblateStaeckelWrapperPotential``.

- ``OblateStaeckelWrapperPotential`` now places its reference curve ``u0`` at
  R = 1 by default (computed from the focal length) rather than on the symmetry
  axis (u0 = 0), where a flattened potential looks nothing like it does in the
  disk. This improves the default Staeckel approximation and changes its
  values.

- Added ``x2deriv`` to 1D (``linearPotential``) potentials and the
  corresponding ``evaluatelinearx2derivs`` function: the second derivative
  d^2 Phi / dx^2, with physical units.

- More array inputs: the ``dehnendf`` and ``shudf`` moments (``surfacemass``,
  ``sigma2surfacemass``, ``sigmaR2``, ``meanvT``, the Oort functions, etc.)
  accept an array of radii; ``rl`` accepts an array of Lz (~300x faster than
  a loop); ``SphericalShellPotential`` and ``HomogeneousSpherePotential``
  accept arrays.

- ``Orbit.from_name`` now falls back on the other SIMBAD mirrors that astroquery
  knows when the configured one cannot be reached (previously, every lookup
  failed whenever the CDS service was down).

- Added wheels for Python 3.15 and Pyodide wheels for Python 3.14. galpy now
  also compiles with GCC 15.

- ``ChandrasekharDynamicalFrictionForce`` and ``FDMDynamicalFrictionForce``:
  construction is ~100x faster for hosts whose potential accepts arrays (e.g.,
  14 s to 0.14 s for ``MWPotential2014``) and orbit integration ~2x faster
  (the force cache never hit). The host's velocity dispersion is now
  tabulated in log r (it was 20-60% wrong at r < 0.03 for common hosts) and
  the Python force uses the same spline as C, so orbits sinking to the center
  integrate identically in C and Python.

- ``Orbit.lyapunov`` is ~100x faster: it no longer forks a process for each of
  its short integration segments (its default is now ``numcores=1``, and
  ``parallel_map`` with ``numcores=1`` now runs serially).

- ``actionAngleIsochroneInverse`` is ~100x faster, solving Kepler's equation
  for all angles at once (identical output).

- ``Orbit.e``, ``rperi``, ``rap``, and ``zmax`` with ``analytic=True`` in a
  spherical potential no longer redo the action-angle setup on every call.

- Fixed a bias in sampling velocities from spherical distribution functions,
  which underestimated the velocity dispersion by ~0.8%; sampled values for a
  fixed random seed change. Sampling also no longer raises ``ValueError: x
  must be increasing`` when the mass converges well inside ``rmax`` (e.g.,
  for ``ExpTruncNFWPotential``).

- Spherical DFs evaluated on an ``Orbit`` instance holding several orbits now
  use each orbit's own angular momentum; before, every orbit was assigned the
  norm of all orbits' L combined, so anisotropic DFs returned wrong values for
  such ``Orbit`` instances.

- ``interpRZPotential`` built from a potential with ``ro``/``vo`` set
  tabulated values in physical units, so its interpolated density, second
  derivatives, and frequencies (and, with ``use_c=False``, its potential and
  forces) were wrong by unit-conversion factors; it now tabulates internal
  units. Its grids are also built ~20x faster for potentials that accept
  arrays.

- ``AnySphericalPotential`` evaluated with an array of radii returned forces
  computed with the enclosed mass at the first radius for all elements, and
  its potential raised an error; both are now evaluated element by element.

- Slicing an ``Orbit`` with per-orbit physical parameters (array ``ro``,
  ``vo``, ``zo``, or solar motion) now selects them along with the orbits
  (previously, e.g., ``orbits[1].R()`` returned values for every ``ro``).
  ``Orbit.toPlanar`` and ``Orbit.toLinear`` now preserve the shape of the
  ``Orbit`` instance (previously flattened).

- Integrating many orbits in C no longer crashes with a segmentation fault
  when the output has more than 2^31-1 elements (e.g., 1e5 3D orbits at 3,600
  times), in all C integrators and in ``actionAngleTorus``'s
  ``xvJacobianFreqs``.

- ``quasiisothermaldf``: guiding-center radii for Lz outside of the
  precomputed grid (Lz < 0.01, including all counter-rotating Lz, or
  Lz > 5 hr vc(5 hr)) are now solved for exactly instead of extrapolated
  (previously up to 60% off, and with the wrong sign for Lz < 0), and
  counter-rotating points inside the grid use the grid at abs(Lz). When the
  actionAngle object raises ``UnboundError`` for an array of points, the DF is
  now evaluated point by point (zero DF for unbound points), which fixes
  ``gl=True`` moments in, e.g., low-mass potentials. ``_precomputerg=False``
  no longer raises an error.

- ``AdiabaticContractionWrapperPotential`` now applies its ``amp`` (which had
  no effect in Python or C), and its potential is offset by the exact integral
  of its force (it was 3e-7 to 5e-7 off).

- The ``sphericaldf`` family now honors a per-call ``ro=``/``vo=`` when
  parsing ``Quantity`` inputs (previously the DF's own ``ro`` and ``vo`` were
  always used).

- ``constantbetadf`` now computes its JAX derivatives in 64-bit precision even
  when JAX's 64-bit mode is off (JAX's default), enabling it only for its own
  calls, and uses the small-radius series of the ``NFWPotential`` and
  ``ExpTruncNFWPotential`` forces. In 32-bit precision the DF was badly wrong
  at the most bound energies (e.g., eight times too small for an NFW
  potential), and the closed-form forces changed the DF there by up to ~5e-5.

- The ``actionAngleStaeckel`` integrals are now regularized at the turning
  points in C and in Python, using a quadrature in an anomaly that absorbs
  their square-root behavior. Actions at the default order are converged to
  ~1e-12 (previously ~5e-4 off; ``c=True`` actions change at that level), and
  C frequencies and angles near the turning points to ~1e-11 (previously up to
  2e-4), stable as the order increases. Orbits that pass close to the axis
  (whose C radial actions were ~5e-5 off, or NaN at high order) use a graded
  rule, and orbits in the midplane without vertical velocity get zmax = 0
  exactly in C (was ~1e-8). The Python path uses the same quadrature mesh as C
  and is ~1.6x faster.

- The C ``actionAngleAdiabatic`` now regularizes its action integrals at the
  turning points (and uses order 20 instead of 10), reducing the errors in the
  actions from ~1e-4 to ~1e-9 (``c=True`` values change; ~30% slower).

- ``actionAngleSpherical`` now handles circular and near-circular orbits,
  where it lost precision, returned NaN, or failed to find a turning point:
  frequencies and angles are accurate to ~1e-9 down to a radial excursion of
  1e-5 of the circular radius, and exactly circular orbits return J_R = 0 and
  the epicycle and circular frequencies. Its azimuthal integrals are now done
  in the inverse radius, improving the azimuthal frequency of nearly radial
  orbits from ~1e-4 to typically ~1e-9. Bound orbits with apocenters beyond
  r = 100 (e.g., eccentricities above 0.98 in a Hernquist potential) are no
  longer declared unbound.

- ``TwoPowerSphericalPotential`` with general (alpha, beta) now computes its
  potential, forces, second derivatives, and enclosed mass from incomplete
  beta functions, in Python and C. The previous closed forms were badly off
  near beta = 3 and at large beta (NaN at large r), especially in C; values
  are now accurate to ~1e-12 or better for 0 <= alpha < 3, 2 < beta <= 180,
  and 1e-12 <= r/a <= 1e8, and C no longer aborts for beta >~ 170.

- ``NFWPotential``, ``BurkertPotential``, and ``TwoPowerSphericalPotential``
  are now accurate to machine precision at small radii (in Python and C), the
  densities and forces of these and of ``EinastoPotential``,
  ``IsochronePotential``, ``LogarithmicHaloPotential``, and
  ``PowerSphericalPotential`` take their limits at r = 0 and r = infinity
  rather than NaN, and ``rE`` is solved to a relative tolerance at small radii.

- ``eddingtondf``'s f(E) is now accurate near the most-bound energy (for NFW it
  was NaN or large and negative there), takes its correct limit at that energy
  (infinite for a cusp), and works with ``rmax=numpy.inf``.

- ``AnyAxisymmetricRazorThinDiskPotential`` is now accurate close to the disk:
  for |z| << R, its vertical force went to zero instead of -2 pi Sigma and its
  potential missed the cusp at the midplane, its second derivatives were NaN or
  infinite at some small |z|, and at z = 0 they were wrong in the 5th to 7th
  digit (they are now evaluated as finite-part integrals).

- ``RazorThinExponentialDiskPotential``'s forces just above |z| = 1e-6 are now
  ~100x more accurate (they were up to 5e-3 off).

- ``EinastoPotential`` with a large index (e.g., the cosmological n = 6) no
  longer returns a zero or wrong radial force and second derivative at the
  radii used in halo modeling, where the enclosed-mass factor rounded to zero.

- ``surfdens`` and ``jeans.sigmar`` integrals are no longer cut short by
  scipy's default absolute tolerance when they are small (e.g., the surface
  density of ``MWPotential2014``'s bulge at R = 1, |z| = 8 was 2e-5 off, and
  sigma_r of an ``SCFPotential`` at r = 25 6e-6 off).

- ``Orbit`` instances can now be evaluated at a time given as a 0-d numpy
  array, which previously raised an error; this broke integrating any orbit in
  a ``MovingObjectPotential`` with ``method='dop853'``.

- Setting up an ``Orbit`` from sky coordinates with ``uvw=True`` no longer
  modifies the input array.

- ``NFWPotential`` set up with ``rmax``/``vmax`` or ``conc``/``mvir`` no
  longer raises an ``AttributeError`` when used with spherical distribution
  functions such as ``eddingtondf``.

- ``actionAngleAdiabaticGrid`` with ``c=False`` no longer raises a
  ``TypeError`` when two or more points fall outside the grid.

- ``actionAngleVerticalInverse`` with a polynomial point transformation
  (``use_pointtransform=True``) now selects tori by their actual action. Each
  torus was labeled by its mean auxiliary action, which differs from the
  actual action (by ~4e-4 at the default degree, more at high energy), so
  requesting a torus's action returned a neighboring torus, and the top
  torus's action crashed the lookup.

- ``Orbit.integrate`` now warns when an orbit leaves an
  ``interpSphericalPotential``'s range also when the potential includes an
  ``FDMDynamicalFrictionForce``.

- An ``AttributeError`` raised inside an actionAngle method is no longer
  reported as "method not implemented".

- ``Orbit.integrate_dxdv`` with a C integrator now gives a base orbit that is
  bit-identical to that of ``Orbit.integrate``.

- ``Orbit.animate`` now defines its button style once per page, avoiding
  duplicated ``<style>`` elements and an HTML error.

v1.12.0 (2026-07-01)
====================

- Added the full 3D variational equations (the state-transition matrix
  dx(t)/dx0, integrated via ``Orbit.integrate_dxdv``) in C for most of the
  potential library, the 3D analogue of the existing planar machinery. This
  enables fast tangent-dynamics, Lyapunov-exponent (see ``Orbit.lyapunov``
  below), and chaos calculations, and lays the groundwork for differentiable
  orbit integration. Many potentials gained their analytic 3D Hessian in C as
  part of this work.

- Added ``Orbit.lyapunov`` to estimate the largest Lyapunov exponent of an orbit
  from the variational equations using the renormalization method of Benettin et
  al. (1980), for both planar and full 3D orbits (using the C variational
  integrators when available). Setting ``spectrum=True`` computes the full
  Lyapunov spectrum instead.

- Extended the 3D variational equations in C to velocity-dependent (dissipative)
  forces, starting with ``ChandrasekharDynamicalFrictionForce`` and
  ``FDMDynamicalFrictionForce``.

- Added the remaining C second derivatives used by these variational equations,
  including ``NonInertialFrameForce`` (so phase-space volumes can be integrated
  in rotating/accelerating frames), the ``OblateStaeckelWrapperPotential`` and
  ``CylindricallySeparablePotentialWrapper`` wrappers, and the
  ``SteadyLogSpiralPotential`` and ``TransientLogSpiralPotential`` spirals.

- Added ``MultipoleExpansionPotential``, which represents the potential of an
  arbitrary (and optionally time-dependent) density distribution via a real
  spherical-harmonic multipole expansion, with both Python and C evaluation of
  the potential, forces, second derivatives, and density. Build it from a density
  function with the ``from_density`` classmethod.

- Added ``DiskMultipoleExpansionPotential``, which represents disk+halo systems
  by solving the Poisson equation with a multipole expansion (the analogue of
  ``DiskSCFPotential`` built on ``MultipoleExpansionPotential``); the two now
  share a ``KuijkenDubinskiDiskExpansionPotential`` base class, with C orbit
  integration support.

- Added a ``StreamTrack`` class holding a smooth, precomputed phase-space track
  through a tidal stream, with accessors for every standard galpy coordinate
  system, a ``cov`` method for the 6x6 phase-space covariance along the track
  (via analytic Jacobians), and plotting with an optional ±σ spread band. Tracks
  can be built from a particle sample (``StreamTrack.from_particles``) or
  directly from a ``streamdf`` or ``streamspraydf`` via their new ``streamTrack``
  methods.

- Added ``ExpTruncNFWPotential``, an NFW profile with an exponential truncation
  ``exp(-r/rc)`` and hence finite total mass, with closed-form mass, potential,
  force, and second derivative. Supports isotropic, Osipkov-Merritt, and
  constant-beta distribution functions and has a C implementation (including the
  full 3D Hessian for the variational equations). A ``from_nfw`` classmethod
  truncates an existing ``NFWPotential``, and the total mass can alternatively be
  set directly with the ``mass=`` keyword.

- Added analytic power-law spherical distribution functions for tracer densities
  nu(r) ~ r^-gamma in power-law potentials (following Evans et al. 1997):
  ``isotropicPowerLawdf``, ``constantbetaPowerLawdf``, and
  ``osipkovmerrittPowerLawdf``, each supporting both the self-consistent and the
  non-self-consistent (tracer != potential-generating density) cases.

- Added support for non-uniform stripping-time distributions (with a pericenter
  helper) and callable, time-dependent ``progenitor_mass`` to the stream spray
  DFs (``streamspraydf``).

- Added support for per-orbit integration time arrays in ``Orbit.integrate``, so
  each orbit in a multi-orbit ``Orbit`` can be integrated over its own time
  range; this also enables batched ``streamspraydf`` integration.

- Added a ``tail=`` keyword to ``fardal15spraydf`` and ``chen24spraydf`` (and
  ``basestreamspraydf``) to select which tidal tail(s) to model (``'leading'``,
  ``'trailing'``, or ``'both'``, the default). This replaces the now-deprecated
  ``leading=`` keyword, which will be removed in v1.14.

- Added an ``align_to_orbit`` helper (to ``galpy.util.coords`` and as an
  ``Orbit`` method) that aligns an orbit horizontally.

- Added a ``cinterp`` option (default ``True``) to ``NonInertialFrameForce``
  that, during C integration, replaces its time-dependent inputs by cubic-spline
  interpolations evaluated natively in C, giving large speed-ups (>100x when the
  inputs are not ``numba``-compatible). Set ``cinterp=False`` to evaluate the
  provided functions directly, which is required for surface-of-section
  integration.

- Vectorized ``EllipsoidalPotential`` evaluations so the triaxial ellipsoidal
  potentials accept array inputs.

- Added frequencies and angles computation for ``actionAngleStaeckel`` in pure
  Python (``c=False``).

- Rewrote the documentation tutorials as tested Jupyter notebooks with gallery
  navigation.

- Fixed a segmentation fault (or silently-dropped force) when integrating planar
  orbits with a C integrator for potentials whose 3D version has a C
  implementation but whose planar version does not (e.g. ``interpRZPotential``,
  ``ChandrasekharDynamicalFrictionForce``); such potentials now fall back to
  Python integration.

- Fixed a stale-force bug in the C implementation of
  ``RotateAndTiltWrapperPotential``, whose force cache was keyed on position only
  and so returned stale forces when re-evaluating a time-dependent wrapped
  potential at the same position but a different time.

- Fixed an incorrect physical-unit conversion for ``phitorque`` in
  ``DissipativeForce`` and ``planarDissipativeForce``.

- Fixed compatibility with astropy >= 8.0, where the ``Galactocentric``
  ``galcen_v_sun`` attribute is now a ``CartesianRepresentation`` rather than a
  ``CartesianDifferential`` (astropy/astropy#18362), so initializing an ``Orbit``
  from a ``SkyCoord`` carrying ``galcen_v_sun`` works again.

- Fixed compatibility with numpy 2.5.0 (complex ``eig``/``eigvals`` results and
  the ``chararray`` deprecation).

- Fixed a crash when sampling a spherical distribution function whose isotropic
  (Eddington-inverted) form dips slightly negative (e.g. near the truncation
  radius of a sharply truncated NFW profile); such regions are now clamped to be
  non-negative and monotonic before sampling.

v1.11.2 (2026-02-27)
====================

- Added CompositePotential, planarCompositePotential, and linearCompositePotential
  classes to represent combinations of multiple potentials in 3D, 2D, and 1D,
  respectively. These classes replace the previous use of lists of potentials
  to represent combined potentials. The new classes provide better performance
  and a cleaner interface for combined potentials. Note that these classes don't
  need to be explicitly instantiated, but instead are created automatically when
  adding potentials using + and, because this was already the recommended way to combine
  potentials, this change is mostly internal. The old way of using lists of potentials
  will be deprecated in a future version (likely v1.14.0).

- Added support for continuing orbit integrations. When an orbit has already
  been integrated and integrate() is called again with a time array that
  continues from the end (for forward continuation) or beginning (for backward
  continuation) of the previous integration, the two integrations are merged.
  This allows integrating an orbit forward and then backward in time, or
  extending an existing integration, without having to re-integrate from the
  initial conditions. The integration times can be obtained using the time()
  method.

- Added frequencies and angles computation for actionAngleAdiabatic in Python.

- Added C implementation for TwoPowerSphericalPotential (including
  HernquistPotential, JaffePotential, and NFWPotential) to enable fast
  orbit integration using the xsf library for hypergeometric functions.

- Added C implementation for TwoPowerTriaxialPotential to enable fast orbit
  integration. The C implementation uses the hyp2f1 hypergeometric function
  and handles the special case where alpha=2.

- Added C implementation of planar second derivatives for
  EllipsoidalPotential, enabling fast variational-equation integration
  for triaxial potentials.

- Added automatic time determination for orbit integration. Orbits can now be
  integrated without specifying an explicit time array by calling integrate(pot),
  which integrates for 10 dynamical times by default. The dynamical time is
  calculated using the potential's tdyn method at the initial position, with
  fallback to 2π·R/vcirc for 2D orbits. This simplifies exploratory orbit analysis
  by providing physics-based default integration times. For composite potentials
  where some components don't support tdyn, galpy automatically filters to working
  components. The feature uses 101 points per dynamical time for good temporal
  resolution.

- Added CylindricallySeparablePotentialWrapper, a wrapper potential that
  constructs a cylindrically-separable potential from a given potential's
  radial and vertical force profiles. Includes a C implementation.

- Added support for Python 3.14.

- Added enhanced ``__repr__`` methods to potential, force, and wrapper
  classes for better display.

- Fixed spherical distribution function sampling for divergent
  potentials/densities.

- Fixed missing boundary term in Eddington inversion calculation.

- Fixed handling of Simbad distances in units other than kpc.

- Auto-detect GSL paths in setup.py to fix compilation failures when
  CFLAGS/LDFLAGS are not set.

- Removed support for Python 3.9 (which has reached its end of life in Oct 2025).

- Added ChandrasekharDynamicalFrictionForce, an implementation of

- Added galpy.df.jeans with tools for Jeans modeling. Currently only

- Added surfdens and evaluateSurfaceDensities method and function,

- Added potentials SphericalShellPotential and RingPotential for the

- Generalized actionAngleStaeckel to allow for different focal lengths
  delta for different phase-space points. Also allowed the order of
  the Gauss-Legendre integration to be specified (default: 10, which

- Allow plotting of arbitrary user-supplied functions of time in
  Orbit.plot, Orbit.plot3d, and Orbit.animate.

v1.2 (2016-09-06)
==================

- Added support for providing inputs to all initializations, methods,
  and functions as Quantities with units and for providing outputs as
  astropy Quantities. See pull request #258 and the documentation for
  full details.

- Added TwoPowerTriaxialPotential, a set of triaxial potentials with
  iso-density contours that are arbitrary, similar, coaxial ellipsoids
  whose 'radial' density is a (different) power-law at small and large
  radii: 1/m^alpha/(1+m)^beta-alpha (the triaxial generalization of
  TwoPowerSphericalPotential, with flattening in the density rather

  fast C implementations that allow efficient orbit integration in
  these potentials (<~5 ms / orbit).

- Added SCFPotential, a class that implements general
  density/potential pairs through the basis expansion approach to
  solving the Poisson equation of Hernquist & Ostriker (1992).  Also
  implemented functions to compute the coefficients for a given
  density function.

- Implemented galpy.actionAngle.actionAngleIsochroneApprox (Bovy 2014)
  for the general case of a time-independent potential, allowing
  action-angle coordinates to be computed for triaxial
  potentials. Previously, this module only supported axisymmetric
  potentials. Also allow the maximum number of terms in the expansion
  to be set object-wide and allow a fixed time-step to be used in the
  necessary orbit integration.

- Added an (experimental) interface to Binney & McMillan's TorusMapper
  code for computing positions and velocities for given actions and
  angles. See the installation instructions for how to properly
  install this.

- Re-defined the amplitude parameters of a few potentials to allow for
  easier setups with amplitudes provided as Quantities with
  units. This is the case for FlattenedPowerPotential,
  PowerSphericalPotential, and PowerSphericalPotentialwCutoff,
  CosmphiDiskPotential, LopsidedDiskPotential, and
  EllipticalDiskPotential.

- Added a module for modeling the effect of a subhalo impact on a
  tidal stream (galpy.df.streamgapdf); see Sanders, Bovy, & Erkal
  (2016). Also includes the fast methods for computing the density
  along the stream and the stream track for a perturbed stream from
  Bovy, Erkal, & Sanders (2016).

- Added functions to galpy.df.streamdf to compute the length of the
  stream, the density along the stream quickly (in various
  coordinates, including custom sky coordinates), to compute the
  probability p(freq_parallel,angle_parallel), and to estimate the
  number of encounters with DM subhalos. Also allow the stream track
  to be computing using the new actionAngleTorus (this last addition
  is still very experimental).

- Added PseudoIsothermalPotential, a standard pseudo-isothermal-sphere
  potential.

- Added KuzminDiskPotential, a potential represented by a razor thin
  disk

- Allow transformations to custom sets of sky coordinates in
  galpy.util.bovy_coords.

- Added the spherical radius as an Orbit method.

- Added the spherical-radial force as a Potential method (rforce) and
  function (evaluaterforces).

- Support for flipping the velocities of an orbit in-place (useful for
  backwards integration).

- quasiisothermaldf input ro replaced by refr to avoid clash with ro
  that specifies units (see above).

- Properly intercept CTRL-C (SIGINT) signals during orbit integration
  in C, allowing long orbit integrations to be interrupted without
  killing an entire Python session.

- Internally use astropy.coordinates transformations to transform
  between (ra,dec) and (l,b). Can be tuned using the astropy-coords
  configuration parameter. Also allow general epochs for the (ra,dec)
  specification.
